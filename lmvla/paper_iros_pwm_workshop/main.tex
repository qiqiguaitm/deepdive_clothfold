\title{When Do World-Model Signals Help VLAs?\\
Two Root Causes, Two One-Line Fixes}

\author{Anonymous submission (non-archival workshop draft v0.1)}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Conditioning vision-language-action (VLA) policies on world-model (WM)
predictions promises task-level guidance for long-horizon manipulation,
yet such signals rarely improve aggregate success, and several recent
systems report that naive injection actively hurts. We present a
diagnosis with per-task resolution. \emph{Phenomenon:} subgoal
conditioning redistributes per-task success --- guidance-limited tasks
gain, others lose --- while every global mixing mechanism we tested
leaves the aggregate on a plateau; a single task can drive an entire
suite-level regression (76\%$\to$42\%). \emph{Root causes:}
(i)~\emph{target redundancy} --- across 1{,}693 episodes, $\sim$95\% of
an absolute future target is explained by the current observation
(residual-to-absolute discriminability $15\times$, 100\% of episodes
consistent); (ii)~\emph{WM-gradient interference} --- prediction
gradients flowing into the shared encoder degrade the policy, shown by
intervention: detaching them recovers a regressed suite from 93.2 to
95.9 without hurting the primary suite. \emph{Fixes, one line each:}
residual targets (subgoal $-$ current) lift LIBERO-10 to
\aggResid$\pm$\aggResidStd\% ($n{=}8$; $+1.55$ over a multi-seed
baseline, Welch $t\!\approx\!2.8$); gradient isolation repairs the
regression. We also report two of our own pre-registered hypotheses
that \emph{failed} --- a retrieval-aliasing account of the single-task
regression and a confusion-score predictability law --- and argue that
task-level units, multi-seed error bars, and pre-registered
falsification lines should become defaults for this literature.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Future-prediction signals are rapidly becoming a standard ingredient of
VLA training: compressed latent future queries (DreamVLA\verify{2507.04447}),
future patch tokens in the policy's own feature space
(AHEAD\verify{2606.02486}), generative future frames as auxiliary
supervision (WorldVLA\verify{2506.21539}), gated visuomotor future tokens
(FutureVLA\verify{2603.10712}), and retrieval-based progress signals
(UR-VC\verify{2607.12892}). Yet a striking pattern runs through this
literature's own ablations: \emph{naive injection hurts}. DreamVLA
reports that depth- or semantics-only future supervision falls below a
no-prediction baseline (``competing gradients dilute the task-relevant
features''); FutureVLA reports that adding future-motor tokens without
gating \emph{drops} success from 62.5\% to 58.4\%; WorldVLA finds that
attending the model's own past actions degrades grasping by 10--50
points unless masked. Third-party benchmarks echo the pattern at the
task level: in WorldArena~2.0\verify{primary source}, the
highest-fidelity predictor scores 100\% on one manipulation task and
0\% on another where a no-prediction baseline reaches 80\%. The field
has responded with local patches --- attention masks, learnable gates,
loss re-weighting --- but without a diagnosis of \emph{why} the signal
harms.

This paper contributes the diagnosis, and a target design derived from
it. Our claims:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A task-level anatomy of the failure} (\S\ref{sec:tension}):
global mixing mechanisms (shared/dedicated queries, guidance scaling,
phase-scheduled dropout) only redistribute per-task success; suite
aggregates hide single-task collapses.
\item \textbf{Root cause I: target redundancy, measured at corpus scale}
(\S\ref{sec:causes}) --- $\sim$95\% of absolute future targets is
current-state re-encoding (1{,}693 episodes, 100\% consistent) --- and
its one-line fix, \emph{residual targets}, absent from all six recent
future-conditioned VLAs we surveyed.
\item \textbf{Root cause II: WM-gradient interference, established by
intervention} (\S\ref{sec:grad}) --- detaching WM prediction gradients
from the shared encoder recovers a regressed suite (93.2$\to$95.9)
without hurting the primary one; this is a controlled confirmation of
the ``competing gradients'' effect reported anecdotally by
DreamVLA\verify{}.
\item \textbf{Negative results under pre-registration}
(\S\ref{sec:falsified}): a retrieval-aliasing mechanism and a
confusion-score predictability law, both falsified by our own
pre-registered tests --- reported in full.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The Precision--Guidance Tension}\label{sec:tension}
\textbf{Setup.} Our primary stack is a dual-channel latent-world-model
VLA: a local channel predicts a fixed short-horizon future ($t{+}7$) in
a frozen visual-semantic feature space, and a global channel predicts a
\emph{milestone} --- a canonical, cross-episode-recurrent future state
mined by retrieval over demonstrations. Both channels condition a
flow-matching action expert. The second stack is a
$\pi_{0.5}$-style VLA\verify{} that consumes the milestone signal as an
extra conditioning token. Full training details in the appendix
\todo{appendix or cite tech report}.

\textbf{Observation.} Table~\ref{tab:tension} summarizes the LIBERO-10
multi-seed picture. Naive milestone injection lifts the hardest
guidance-limited task (t6, a two-object sequencing task) while
degrading precision-limited tasks (t7, t8); dedicated query capacity
(2Q) restores t8 but starves the guidance channel (t6 drops); guidance
scaling (CFG) and diffusion-phase-scheduled dropout each shift the

A qualitatively similar trade appears on a second, $\pi_{0.5}$-style

quantitative law --- see \S\ref{sec:falsified}.

\section{Two Root Causes}\label{sec:causes}
\subsection{Root cause I: target redundancy}
Absolute subgoal targets are dominated by the present. Across the full
corpus (1{,}693 episodes, four suites), $\sim$95\% of the target's
energy is explained by the current state --- consistent in 100\% of
episodes --- and the along-trajectory discriminability of the residual
component $\mathrm{ms}-h_t$ exceeds that of the absolute target by
$\sim$15$\times$ (per-episode examples: CV \residCV{} vs.\ \absCV).
Architecturally the redundancy is double: the conditioning stream
already contains the current tokens $h_t$, so an absolute target
re-encodes information that is already present --- at best wasted
capacity, at worst competing gradients, exactly the failure DreamVLA's
ablation describes\verify{}.

\subsection{A single task drives a suite-level regression}
\label{sec:anatomy}
The LIBERO-spatial suite provides a natural experiment. Under absolute
milestone targets the suite regresses by $-3.0$ points --- but per-task
resolution shows the regression is \emph{entirely one task}
(Fig.~\ref{tab:anatomy}): t5, ``pick up the black bowl \emph{on} the
ramekin,'' collapsing from 76\% to 42\% (18\% in an independently
retrained model), while the other nine tasks are unaffected (97--100\%
for every variant). Residual targets partially rescue t5 (52\%).

We initially attributed this to cross-episode retrieval aliasing (t5
shares its object set with two near-duplicate tasks). A pre-registered
confusion-score test \emph{falsified} that account
(\S\ref{sec:falsified}). The intervention that does repair the suite is
\emph{gradient isolation} (\S\ref{sec:grad}), implicating training-time
interference rather than retrieval confusion.

\section{The Fix: Residual Targets}\label{sec:fix}
The global channel's ground truth becomes
$h^{\mathrm{ms}}_{\mathrm{gt}} = \mathrm{ms} - h_t$
instead of $\mathrm{ms}$. The channel's prediction (now a delta)
conditions the action expert alongside the untouched current tokens
$h_t$ --- a non-redundant decomposition: $h_t$ carries ``where I am,''
the channel carries only ``what must change.'' The change is one line,
parameter-free, and training/inference-consistent. Each element of the
final design answers one diagnosed cause: dual parallel channels
(tension: never overwrite the local precision channel), residual
targets (redundancy), dedicated queries (capacity), and --- suggested
by our anatomy but left to future work --- retrieval-confidence gating
(aliasing).

\section{Root Cause II: WM-Gradient Interference, and Its Fix}
\label{sec:grad}
The WM prediction heads are trained through the same encoder that
serves the policy. An A/B intervention isolates the effect: detaching
the WM-prediction gradients from the shared encoder (one line) recovers
the regressed spatial suite from $93.2$ to $95.9$ --- near the no-WM
level of $96.5$ --- while leaving LIBERO-10 unharmed. The conditioning
\emph{signal} is preserved; only the gradient path changes. This turns
the ``competing gradients dilute task-relevant features'' observation
that DreamVLA\verify{} reports anecdotally into a controlled,
per-suite-verified mechanism, and reattributes our single-task
regression (\S\ref{sec:anatomy}) to training-time interference.

\section{What We Falsified (Pre-Registered)}
\label{sec:falsified}
Two of our own hypotheses died by their pre-registered criteria, and we
report them as results. (1)~\emph{Retrieval-aliasing mechanism}: we
predicted the collapsed task t5 would be an extreme outlier in a
cross-episode confusion score; it is not, and the most-confused task
triplet in another suite shows zero harm. (2)~\emph{Predictability law}:
we predicted per-task deltas would correlate with data-computable
confusion/structure scores; the correlation does not survive
(a difficulty--harm correlation on a second stack likewise loses
significance once a single outlier task is removed). These negatives
sharpen the paper's scope: the defensible root causes are redundancy
and gradient interference --- both established by measurement or
intervention, not narrative.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
\textbf{Protocol.} Multi-seed everywhere (population std reported;
$n$ per row); aggregate differences below 1.5 points are never claimed
(per-seed binomial noise makes smaller aggregate deltas
unattributable); pre-registered predictions with falsification lines
were logged before each decisive evaluation \todo{cite repo/appendix}.

\textbf{LIBERO-10.} Residual targets give the only variant that clears
the claim line: \aggResid$\pm$\aggResidStd\% ($n{=}8$) versus
\aggBaselineArmB$\pm$0.97 ($n{=}4$), $\Delta{=}{+}1.55$, Welch
$t\!\approx\!2.8$. The shape matches the diagnosis: guidance tasks rise
(t6 $82.5\to$\tSixResid, $+5.0$; t9 $86.0\to90.8$, $+4.8$), precision
tasks do not fall (t7 $100\pm0$; t8 within its own large variance).
Notably, an earlier pre-registered prediction for the spatial rescue
(``$\geq$95'') was \emph{missed} (94.4): we report this as a partial
confirmation --- the mechanism is directionally right and
quantitatively incomplete, which is precisely what motivates the
two-component harm model of \S\ref{sec:anatomy}.

\textbf{RoboTwin (blocks family, 6 tasks).} With identical recipes and
training steps, milestone conditioning turns a $46.4\pm0.8$ baseline
into $49.3\pm1.5$ ($n{=}4$, $t{=}3.1$); the gain concentrates in the
one multi-stage timing task (beat-block-hammer, $0.5\%\to20.0\%$),
while saturated tasks are flat and two zero-competence tasks (stacking)
remain at 0\% for both --- subgoal signals need a competent base to
have leverage. \emph{Caveat (honest):} the shared baseline is
under-trained on this family relative to a single-task reference
(90\%); the A/B contrast is valid, absolute numbers are not

\section{Related Work}
Future-signal injection spans four families: appended queries with
structured attention masks (DreamVLA\verify{}), future-token
replacement with uncertainty-truncated adaptive horizons
(AHEAD\verify{} --- the only adaptive-horizon design we are aware of),
auxiliary-loss-only supervision (WorldVLA\verify{}, Spatial
Forcing\verify{2510.12276}), and gated cross-attention
(FutureVLA\verify{}); retrieval-based progress correction (UR-VC)
forms a fifth, scalar, line. All predict \emph{absolute} futures; task-level
analysis stops at the suite granularity in all of them. Their own
ablations (competing gradients; naive-injection drops; action-history
contamination) are, in our reading, three independent encounters with
the redundancy/aliasing causes we isolate --- patched respectively with
masks, gates, and masking, i.e., treatments for a disease that had not
been named.

\section{Discussion and Open Problems}
(1)~\emph{The two fixes compose.} Residual targets act on what the
channel predicts; gradient isolation acts on how it trains --- their
combination is our current experiment. (2)~\emph{Control-limited tasks do
not respond to subgoals.} Our hardest precision task fails in
grasp/place execution regardless of guidance --- consistent with this
workshop's open problem of long-horizon force-control stability; the
lever there is control-side (e.g., prefix-continuity replanning), which
we find composes additively with signal-side fixes. (3)~\emph{Prediction
quality is not downstream utility.} The WorldArena~2.0 reversal
(best-fidelity predictor: 100\% on one task, 0\% where a no-prediction
baseline gets 80\%) mirrors our t5 anatomy; both argue that the field's
bottleneck is \emph{what} to predict, not how well. (4)~\emph{Claim
discipline.} Multi-seed error bars, task-level units, and
pre-registered falsification lines changed our own conclusions twice
(a single-seed baseline mirage; a partial-rescue verdict); we suggest
they become community defaults for this literature.
