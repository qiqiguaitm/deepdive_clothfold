World-model-augmented robot policies condition actions on predicted future
visual states, but latent targets can drift when a vision--language--action
(VLA) encoder remains trainable.  We introduce \textbf{MINT-VLA}
(\textbf{M}ilestone \textbf{I}ntegration with \textbf{N}ative-Space
\textbf{T}argets for \textbf{V}ision--\textbf{L}anguage--\textbf{A}ction
models), which mines recurring task stages, recomputes their targets in the
current policy encoder space, and injects one predicted prefix token.  Its
$\pi_{0.5}$ adapter adds 0.25\% parameters, 18 MiB peak training memory, and no
measurable inference-latency penalty.  Held-out latent cosine improves from
0.7479 for persistence to 0.8134.  Yet, at fixed checkpoints on 518 common
scenes, correct predictions do not outperform current, zero, permuted,
cross-task, or within-task controls after multiplicity correction.  A
three-seed oracle-transition arm changes no-hint success by $+0.53$ points
(95\% CI [$-2.06$,$+3.00$]) and fails its utility gate.
Across three matched training seeds and 10,800 frozen-scene rollouts, no hint
reaches 79.39\%, offline absolute conditioning 75.44\%, and MINT-VLA 70.81\%.
Their paired hierarchical differences from no hint are $-3.94$ points (95\% CI
[$-6.58$,$-0.97$]) and $-8.58$ points ([$-11.47$,$-5.75$]), respectively.
MINT-VLA regresses on all six tasks; offline absolute conditioning improves
only Stack-2 by 0.50 points.  Thus future-state predictability, representation
alignment, and low overhead do not establish control utility for the tested
native-space milestone interfaces.
