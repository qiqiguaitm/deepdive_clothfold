Predicted future representations are increasingly used to condition
vision--language--action (VLA) policies, but representation accuracy does not
show that a policy uses the prediction or benefits from it.  We introduce a
temporal-contract audit and an evidence ladder that separate future-state
predictability, matched training utility, fixed-checkpoint content use, and
component attribution.  A source audit shows that the released LaWAM RoboTwin
target coincides with its 36-action execution endpoint, whereas recurrence
milestones are variable-horizon targets and only 12.50\% fall within the
executed prefix.  At the frozen released LaWAM checkpoint, normal prediction
reaches 94.00\% success versus 40.33\% when future content is shuffled within
task, a paired difference of +53.67 points (95\% CI
[$+36.08$,$+68.58$]); all six task effects are positive.  This establishes
content use at that checkpoint, not which training component produced it.  Two
counterexamples delimit the claim.  MINT-VLA improves held-out future-feature
cosine from 0.7479 to 0.8134 yet lowers matched control by 8.58 points.  A new
policy-preserving PredictiveActionAdapter learns action-conditioned future
grids with 0.50\% additional parameters, but its independently matched
three-seed effect is +1.94 points (95\% CI [$-5.78$,$+12.75$]) and its
normal--shuffled contrast is unresolved.  Predicted futures can constrain VLA
control, but neither predictability, temporal proximity, nor a one-seed gain
alone identifies a reliable mechanism.
