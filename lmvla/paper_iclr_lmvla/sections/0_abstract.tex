World-model-augmented robot policies condition actions on predicted future
visual states, but latent targets can drift while a vision--language--action
(VLA) encoder trains.  We introduce \textbf{MINT-VLA}
(\textbf{M}ilestone \textbf{I}ntegration with \textbf{N}ative-Space
\textbf{T}argets for \textbf{V}ision--\textbf{L}anguage--\textbf{A}ction
models), which mines recurring stages, recomputes targets in the current
encoder space, and injects one predicted prefix token.  Held-out latent cosine
improves from 0.7479 for persistence to 0.8134.  Across three matched training
seeds and 10,800 frozen-scene rollouts, no hint reaches 79.39\%, offline
absolute conditioning 75.44\%, and MINT-VLA 70.81\%.  Their paired differences
from no hint are $-3.94$ points (95\% CI [$-6.58$,$-0.97$]) and $-8.58$ points
([$-11.47$,$-5.75$]).  Correct predictions also fail to outperform current,
zero, permuted, or cross-task controls, while a three-seed oracle-transition
arm changes success by $+0.53$ points ([$-2.06$,$+3.00$]).  Separately, a
policy-preserving predictive adapter improves one fixed matched $\pi_{0.5}$
A0 by 11.61 points across three candidate seeds, adding 0.50\% parameters and
0.93\,ms direct-model latency.  Independently matched A0 training reverses
this conclusion: the three seed effects are $+13.42$, $-5.50$, and $-2.08$
points, with mean $+1.94$ and 95\% CI [$-5.78$,$+12.75$].  Its matched
normal--shuffled effect is also only $+0.53$ points
([$-2.14$,$+3.08$]).  Future-state predictability, representation alignment,
and a one-seed gain therefore do not ensure replicated control utility.
