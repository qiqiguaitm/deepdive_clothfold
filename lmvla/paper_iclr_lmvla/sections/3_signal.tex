\section{Problem Setting and Controlled Comparisons}\label{sec:setup}
Let $h_t=E_\theta(o_t)$ be the current policy representation, $y_t$ the feature
of a future milestone frame, and $q_t$ the token presented to the action policy:
\begin{equation}
  a_t=\pi_\psi(h_t,q_t,l),
\end{equation}
where $l$ is the task instruction.  When $E_\theta$ changes during fine-tuning,
future conditioning introduces four separable design choices.

\paragraph{Target selection.}
A fixed horizon, terminal frame, random future, or recurrent milestone encodes
a different claim about which future state is useful.

\paragraph{Representation space.}
The target may come from an external encoder, an earlier policy encoder, or
the current encoder.  A projection matches dimensions but does not align
semantic neighborhoods, and saved features do not follow encoder drift.

\paragraph{Parameterization and forward path.}
The model may predict an absolute feature $y_t$ or a displacement
$y_t-h_t$, and may inject that prediction into the policy or use it only as an
auxiliary objective.  A residual target can emphasize change without being a
natural standalone conditioning input.

\paragraph{Backward path.}
With milestone loss
$\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\rm act}+\lambda\mathcal L_{\rm ms}$, the auxiliary
gradient can reshape shared current features.  A performance change may thus
come from forward guidance, representation shaping, or both.
