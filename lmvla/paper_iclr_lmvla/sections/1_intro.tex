\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
World models represent environment dynamics by predicting future observations
or latent states from the current state and, in many formulations, an action.
Recent vision--language--action (VLA) systems use predicted images, latent
observation features, or visual subgoals to inform action generation
\citep{vpp2024,susie2024,ahead2026,futurevla2026}.  Prediction quality alone,
however, does not establish an improvement in control.  WorldArena reports a
correlation of only $r{=}0.360$ between world-model quality and planning performance
\citep{worldarena2026}, while VLA prediction systems use specialized attention,
alignment, or gating before future supervision reaches the controller
\citep{dreamvla2025,futurevla2026,deltavla2026}.  The unresolved problem is
therefore not only how to predict a future state, but how to integrate the
prediction into a pretrained action policy.

This integration problem is especially important when the VLA visual encoder remains trainable.
An independent visual world model preserves its own representation but repeats
perception or generation at deployment.  A precomputed latent is cheaper, yet
an external encoder can organize the scene differently from the policy.  Even
a feature computed by the policy encoder becomes outdated as fine-tuning
changes that encoder.
Finally, an auxiliary future loss can alter shared current features whether or
not the policy uses the predicted content.  Encoder mismatch, target drift,
forward conditioning, and backward representation shaping are different
causes; a method-versus-baseline comparison cannot identify them.

Here we present \textbf{MINT-VLA} (\textbf{M}ilestone \textbf{I}ntegration with
\textbf{N}ative-Space \textbf{T}argets for
\textbf{V}ision--\textbf{L}anguage--\textbf{A}ction models), a compact adapter
that defines milestone targets in the policy's current visual space
(Fig.~\ref{fig:hero}).  MINT-VLA first finds stage states that recur across
successful demonstrations and stores the corresponding frames.  During policy
training, the current encoder represents both the observation and a
stop-gradient milestone target.  A two-layer predictor estimates their
displacement and reconstructs an absolute milestone feature.  An
architecture-specific projection passes this feature through the VLA's native
conditioning input; our $\pi_{0.5}$ implementation appends one prefix token.
At deployment, the predictor operates
on the current observation; it requires no retrieval, target encoder, or image
decoder.

The completed experiments separate future-state predictability from control
utility.  On 480 held-out episodes, the predictor reaches latent cosine 0.8134
versus 0.7479 for persistence.  Yet at fixed $\pi_{0.5}$ A2 and A3 checkpoints,
correct future content fails to outperform current, zero, feature-permuted,
cross-task, or within-task controls on 518 common scenes after Holm correction.
Nine supporting six-task LaWAM comparisons give the same null result.
Preserving a privileged milestone as a
$4\!\times\!4$ patch grid does not repair the interface: it fails to beat both
no-goal and current-image controls on Stack-3 and significantly increases the
Hammer action endpoint error.  Thus neither an empty predictor nor one-token
spatial compression alone explains the control gap.  An exact-paired public
$\pi_{0.5}$ reaches 78.17\% through the same evaluator.  Across three matched corrected
training seeds, no hint reaches 79.39\%, offline absolute conditioning 75.44\%,
and MINT-VLA 70.81\%.  Relative to no hint, their paired hierarchical
differences are $-3.94$ points (95\% CI [$-6.58$,$-0.97$]) and $-8.58$ points
([$-11.47$,$-5.75$]), respectively.  MINT-VLA is lower on all six tasks, while
offline absolute conditioning improves only Stack-2 by 0.50 points.  The
corrected result therefore supports an integration diagnosis, not a
method-benefit claim.  A predeclared three-seed follow-up reaches the same
boundary with privileged stage transitions: its mean effect over A0 is
$+0.53$ points (95\% CI [$-2.06$,$+3.00$]), with a negative effect at seed
1002 and regressions on Hammer and both ranking tasks.

We also evaluate a separate repair hypothesis that preserves the pretrained
$\pi_{0.5}$ pathways and confines an action-conditioned predictive adapter to
the action expert.  Its preregistered seed-1000 screen improves over a matched
current-source A0 by 13.42 points (82.42\% versus 69.00\%) without a task-level
regression.  The frozen P2 replication yields candidate effects of +13.42,
+9.08, and +12.33 points across training seeds, with a mean +11.61-point
effect and hierarchical 95\% interval [$+8.31$,$+14.67$].  This establishes
candidate-seed replication relative to the fixed matched A0.  Once A0 is
independently trained at all three seeds, however, the matched effects are
+13.42, -5.50, and -2.08 points; their mean is +1.94 points with hierarchical
95\% CI [$-5.78$,$+12.75$].  The adapter also lacks content-specific
causality: across three seeds the margin over shuffled action
conditioning is $+0.53$ points (95\% CI [$-2.14$,$+3.08$]; Holm-adjusted
$p{=}0.534$).  Adding recurrence-derived CRAVE
targets reduces success to 62.92\%; this rejects that auxiliary extension
rather than the parent predictive adapter.

A separate demonstration-weighting screen gives a second bounded positive
direction.  At seed 1000, terminal-outcome weighting reaches 77.58\%, versus
74.25\% for ordinary fine-tuning and 71.08\% for outcome-free CRAVE weighting.
The predeclared three-seed replication retains positive mean differences of
2.81 and 3.81 points, but both hierarchical 95\% intervals cross zero.  We
therefore reject a replicated utility claim rather than reinterpret these
sample weights as a value or control-utility mechanism.

This paper makes three contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We introduce a milestone target recomputed by the current policy encoder
  and an adapter that uses each VLA's native conditioning input; the evaluated
  $\pi_{0.5}$ implementation adds one token and 0.25\% parameters.
  \item We define paired content interventions and a privileged spatial gate
  that distinguish prediction quality, content use, and interface capacity.
  \item We show across three matched training seeds that a nontrivial future
  predictor need not improve control: both conditioned arms regress, correct
  content has no resolved advantage, and a privileged oracle-transition route
  also fails its replicated closed-loop utility gate.  A separately scoped
  policy-preserving adapter passes a candidate-seed gate against one fixed A0,
  but fails the independent matched-A0 gate and its content-specific
  intervention remains unresolved.
  Outcome-calibrated weighting also passes a one-seed directional screen, but
  its completed three-seed gate rejects a replicated method effect.
\end{itemize}
