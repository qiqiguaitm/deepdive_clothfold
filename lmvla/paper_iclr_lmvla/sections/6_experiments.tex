\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Future targets obey different temporal contracts}
The audited released LaWAM target is the endpoint of both its 36-action model
window and its 36-action executed prefix.  The historical local contract instead
predicts the endpoint of a 50-action model window but executes 36 actions before
replanning.  Recurrence targets are farther away: their mean horizon is 117.45
frames, with task means from 53.64 for Hammer to 130.44 for Stack-3.  Only
12.50\% of 420,238 pairs lie within the executed prefix, and consecutive frames
share the same target 99.42\% of the time.  Thus the fixed endpoint supplies a
local boundary condition, whereas the mined target usually denotes a persistent
multi-query stage.  These measurements motivate a temporal-grounding
hypothesis but do not show which target improves control.

\subsection{The released LaWAM policy uses the predicted endpoint content}
Correct future content is necessary for the frozen released LaWAM checkpoint.
Normal inference succeeds on 94.00\% of 1,200 episodes, compared with 40.33\%
under the prespecified within-task shuffle (Fig.~\ref{fig:content}).  The paired
difference is +53.67 points, with hierarchical 95\% CI
[$+36.08$,$+68.58$] and Holm-adjusted exact McNemar
$p=6.75\!\times\!10^{-180}$.  All six task effects are positive, from +17.0
points on Hammer to +75.0 on Stack-3, and all four evaluation-seed effects are
positive.

The route controls agree with the content intervention.  Null content reaches
35.17\%, giving normal--null +58.83 points (95\% CI
[$+36.83$,$+79.50$]), while persistence reaches 24.42\%, giving
normal--persistence +69.58 points ([$+48.00$,$+87.25$]).  Both Holm-adjusted
tests pass, and every task effect is positive.  Together these comparisons show
that this fixed policy depends on the particular predicted endpoint grid, not
only on the presence of an extra route or a repeated current representation.
They do not identify which training component created that dependence.

\subsection{Execution cadence does not explain the historical local result}
The cadence intervention does not detect a local-WM-specific response.  At
$E=36$ and $E=50$, future-off succeeds at 91.50\% and 88.42\%, while local-WM
succeeds at 88.08\% and 86.42\%.  The prespecified difference-in-differences is
+1.42 points with hierarchical 95\% CI [$-3.00$,$+5.92$].  Task effects are
mixed: +5.5 points for Hammer and +6.5 for ranking-RGB, but negative for
Handover and both stacking tasks.  This one-training-seed diagnostic rejects
the cadence-sensitivity gate.  It neither proves that long-horizon milestones
are ineffective nor that endpoint alignment causes LaWAM's content use.

\subsection{Predictability does not rescue MINT-VLA control}
MINT-VLA predicts a nontrivial future representation.  Across 480 held-out
episodes, latent cosine is 0.8134 versus 0.7479 for persistence, with 46.9\%
top-1 and 80.8\% top-5 target retrieval.  The matched control result moves in
the opposite direction.  Across three training seeds, no hint reaches 79.39\%,
an offline absolute milestone token 75.44\%, and MINT-VLA 70.81\%.  Relative to
no hint, the effects are $-3.94$ points (95\% CI
[$-6.58$,$-0.97$]) and $-8.58$ points
([$-11.47$,$-5.75$]), respectively.  MINT-VLA regresses on all six tasks.
At fixed MINT and offline-milestone checkpoints, correct targets also fail to
outperform current, zero, permuted, within-task, or cross-task replacements
after Holm correction.

Privileged targets do not provide a positive exception.  Oracle stage
transitions change success by +0.53 points across three training seeds (95\%
CI [$-2.06$,$+3.00$]); a correct next-subtask prompt trails no prompt on a
fixed public policy; and a ground-truth $4\times4$ milestone grid fails its
two-task offline action gate.  These controls remove predictor error, semantic
compression, or global pooling separately.  They do not establish that a
distant milestone is sufficient to constrain the next action chunk.

\subsection{PredictiveActionAdapter is controlled but not independently replicated}
The adapter learns action-dependent future grids.  On 8,192 held-out pairs,
patch cosine is 0.996015 under normal actions, 0.992406 under temporal reversal,
and 0.993263 under masked actions.  Normal--reversed is 0.003588 (episode-level
95\% CI [0.003481,0.003698]).  The final checkpoint changes none of 51 inherited
parameter leaves, and the policy route starts at exactly zero.  This establishes
an action-conditioned representation and an isolated initialization, not
control utility.

The control effect is highly sensitive to baseline training seed.  The
candidate scores 82.42\%, 78.08\%, and 81.33\% at seeds 1000--1002.  Comparing
all candidates with only the seed-1000 A0 score of 69.00\% gives an apparent
+11.61-point mean improvement.  Independently trained A0s at the same seeds
score 69.00\%, 83.58\%, and 83.42\%; the matched effects therefore become
+13.42, $-5.50$, and $-2.08$ points (Table~\ref{tab:predictive-p3}).  Their mean
is +1.94 points with hierarchical 95\% CI [$-5.78$,$+12.75$].  The utility gate
fails, and regressions of 13 points on ranking-size, 9 points on Stack-3, and
5.5 points on ranking-RGB violate task safety.

The mechanism panel is also unresolved.  Across the same three checkpoints,
normal--reversed action content is +0.53 points (95\% CI
[$-2.14$,$+3.08$], Holm-adjusted $p=0.534$); normal--zero-route is +1.50
points ([$-1.31$,$+4.39$], adjusted $p=0.205$); and normal--masked is +1.33
points ([$-2.22$,$+4.86$], adjusted $p=0.224$).  The adapter adds 0.50\%
parameters, 0.93\,ms direct-model latency, 2.76\,ms WebSocket latency, and
18\,MiB matched peak training memory.  Low overhead and action-conditioned
prediction do not override the failed utility, safety, and content-use gates.

\subsection{Source attribution remains open}
TG1A establishes content use for one released checkpoint, but its training
package contains pretrained predictive components, downstream auxiliary
losses, and inference conditioning.  These sources cannot be separated by
turning the future grid off only at inference.  The frozen TG4 design therefore
compares clean base, future-off, auxiliary-only, conditioning-only,
parameter-matched null, and full training at three seeds.
