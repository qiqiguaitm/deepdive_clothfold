\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Predictive representations for robot policies.}
Prior systems predict future images, videos, latent observation features, or
visual subgoals.  VPP conditions inverse dynamics on future visual
representations produced by a video diffusion model \citep{vpp2024}; SuSIE
generates image subgoals for a goal-conditioned controller and compares them
with ground-truth future images \citep{susie2024}.  WorldVLA predicts future
images together with actions, whereas DreamVLA forecasts dynamic, spatial, and
semantic scene information for action prediction
\citep{dreamvla2025,worldvla2025}.  AHEAD predicts future patches in a frozen
OpenVLA feature space \citep{ahead2026}.  FutureVLA learns joint visuomotor
representations and transfers them to downstream VLAs through latent alignment
\citep{futurevla2026}.  Concurrent $\Delta$VLA models changes relative to the
current scene \citep{deltavla2026}.  LaWAM predicts action-conditioned future
observation features as latent visual subgoals; WLA-0 jointly predicts subtasks,
images, and actions; and Enfold distills a generative world model into a
representation computed from the current observation
\citep{lawam2026,wla02026,enfold2026}.  MINT-VLA therefore does not claim the first
latent future state, visual subgoal, residual predictor, or auxiliary prediction
loss.  It addresses a specific integration problem: keeping target features
consistent with a trainable policy encoder and mapping the prediction to the
VLA's existing conditioning interface.

\paragraph{Milestone selection.}
States shared across successful trajectories have long been used as
bottlenecks or options \citep{simsek2009,options1999}.  MINT-VLA uses
cross-episode visual recurrence to select candidate stage states without
manually annotated boundaries.  This differs from a fixed temporal horizon and
from terminal-goal conditioning, but recurrence itself is not sufficient
evidence that the selected state is useful.  Our fixed-horizon,
shuffled-action, masked-action, terminal, and oracle controls follow SuSIE's
principle of separating subgoal quality from controller capacity.

\paragraph{Integration and negative transfer.}
Auxiliary objectives can improve or damage a shared representation depending
on weighting and task \citep{gradsim2018,olaux2019,pcgrad2020}; negative
transfer is not explained by gradient conflict alone
\citep{forkmerge2023,lyle2021aux}.  VLA systems accordingly use different
interfaces, including structured attention, latent alignment, conditional
variation modules, or a frozen policy feature space
\citep{dreamvla2025,futurevla2026,deltavla2026,ahead2026}.  Selective gradient
routing is also used to preserve pretrained VLA semantics
\citep{knowledgeinsulation2025}.  MINT-VLA's current implementation stops the
milestone target branch but still allows its loss to update current visual
features through the predictor.  Because the matched utility gate fails, we do
not attribute the regression to one gradient path; the content and privileged
controls instead test whether the supplied condition is action-sufficient.

\paragraph{Establishing control benefit.}
Prediction quality and control performance can diverge
\citep{worldarena2026,robowmbench2026,wmevalposition2026}, and correlated
conditioning can induce causal confusion \citep{causalconfusion2019,copycat2020}.
Evaluation seeds also do not replace independent training seeds
\citep{henderson2018,rliable2021}.  We therefore require closed-loop success,
fixed-checkpoint content interventions, task-level reporting, and training-seed
replication.  These controls are necessary to determine whether performance
depends on the predicted future state rather than merely on an additional
visual token.
