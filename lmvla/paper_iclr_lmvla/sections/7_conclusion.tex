\section{Discussion and Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
The current results expose a gap between future-state predictability and
control utility.  MINT-VLA predicts held-out milestone representations better
than persistence, yet correct future content does not outperform five control
conditions at fixed $\pi_{0.5}$ checkpoints on 518 common scenes.  Nine
supporting six-task LaWAM comparisons are also null, and their only
multiplicity-corrected task-level difference is adverse.  Better retrieval
content leaves control unchanged.  These interventions therefore reject the
claim that the tested one-token policies improve because they use the correct
predicted future.

Changing the representation alone does not repair this gap.  Explicit correct
next-subtask prompts reach 77.08\% on a fixed public $\pi_{0.5}$ checkpoint,
below no subtask at 80.83\% and indistinguishable from within-task shuffled
semantics; a 12.5-point Stack-3 gain is accompanied by a 25.0-point Handover
regression.  One-token compression is also not a sufficient explanation.  A
topology-preserving privileged $4\!\times\!4$ milestone fails to beat both
no-goal and current-image controls on Stack-3 and significantly increases
Hammer endpoint error.  Because this probe supplies the true future target, it
removes predictor error from the comparison.  Its failure points to a deeper
action-sufficiency problem: a later visual state can identify a task stage
without specifying the path, contact transition, arm assignment, or timing
needed for the next action.  This interpretation is consistent with the
Handover result, where a same-task foreign hint is less harmful than the
correct future.

These findings have a deliberately bounded scope.  The spatial probe is a
1,000-update offline action-flow experiment on two tasks, not a closed-loop
test of every spatial or generative subgoal interface.  Methods that generate
instruction-aligned subgoals, condition predictions on candidate actions, or
refresh targets online pose different hypotheses.  Our results instead show
that representation alignment, prediction accuracy, and low deployment cost
are individually insufficient evidence for control benefit.

Outcome-linked recurrence statistics sharpen this interpretation without
changing it.  In a frozen 120-episode rollout panel, progress, regression, and
stall statistics separate successful and failed trajectories, but the
regression, stall, and low-density alarms have success false-positive rates of
38.4\%, 89.9\%, and 99.0\%.  The representation therefore contains diagnostic
information, but neither this correlation nor the semantic prompt screen
establishes a usable value function or control interface.

The corrected three-seed comparison supports the diagnostic branch.  Across
10,800 frozen-scene rollouts, A0 reaches 79.39\%, A2-Abs 75.44\%, and A3
70.81\%.  Relative to matched A0 seeds, the paired hierarchical differences
are $-3.94$ points for A2-Abs (95\% CI [$-6.58$,$-0.97$]) and $-8.58$ points for
A3 ([$-11.47$,$-5.75$]).  A3 regresses on all six tasks and A2-Abs improves
only Stack-2 by 0.50 points.  The tested native-space milestone interfaces
therefore do not improve control under the corrected $\pi_{0.5}$ recipe.  A
separate privileged oracle-transition arm also fails its predeclared gate:
its difference from A0 is $+0.53$ points (95\% CI [$-2.06$,$+3.00$]), seed
1002 reverses direction, and three task means regress despite positive results
on the three multistage tasks.  We close the tracker, selector,
mature-initialization, task-expansion, and second-VLA gates rather than tune
them against these replicated negative results.

These failures do not make every method proposed in this study ineffective.
The independently designed, policy-preserving predictive adapter has direct
positive closed-loop evidence at seed 1000: 82.42\% versus 69.00\% for its
matched A0, with positive differences on all six tasks.  The result remains a
screen because the action-shuffled control reaches 81.17\% and the
multiplicity-adjusted contrast is unresolved.  The failed recurrence-aligned
R1 model is likewise evidence against that auxiliary extension, not against
the parent adapter.  We therefore retain the original negative integration
conclusion, report the P1 result as bounded one-seed evidence, and defer any
replicated predictive-adapter utility claim to the frozen P2 analysis.
