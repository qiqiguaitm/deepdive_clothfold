\section{Discussion and Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The experiments resolve one important ambiguity: a predicted future can be
causally relevant to VLA control.  Replacing LaWAM's normal endpoint grid with
shuffled, null, or persistent content sharply reduces success at the same
checkpoint and on the same scenes.  This result is stronger than a system-level
score because it identifies dependence on the particular predicted content.
It is narrower than a method-level improvement claim because the checkpoint's
pretraining, auxiliary objectives, and inference route were learned together.

The contrast with MINT-VLA and PredictiveActionAdapter explains why prediction
metrics are insufficient.  MINT-VLA predicts its target but supplies a pooled,
variable-horizon stage through the VLM prefix; matched control deteriorates.
PredictiveActionAdapter supplies an action-conditioned grid residual directly
to the action expert and preserves the base policy at initialization; its
matched effect nevertheless varies from +13.42 to $-5.50$ points across seeds,
and reversing proposal content has no resolved effect.  These outcomes do not
invalidate the architectures.  They show that representational dependence,
optimization stability, task safety, and content causality are separate method
requirements.

Temporal grounding remains a plausible but unconfirmed explanation.  The
released LaWAM target coincides with the executed chunk endpoint, whereas most
recurrence milestones lie several policy queries ahead and carry no time-to-go.
Such a milestone may identify eventual progress without specifying contact,
path, arm assignment, or how far the next chunk should move.  A hierarchical
controller could translate it into a local endpoint.  However, the completed
cadence intervention does not detect local-WM-specific sensitivity, and the
matched target comparison was rejected before evaluation on data-order
integrity.  We therefore do not claim that horizon mismatch caused the MINT or
LMWM results.

The study is limited to simulated RoboTwin tasks, the evaluated $\pi_{0.5}$ and
LaWAM interfaces, three training seeds where training comparisons are
available, and one released checkpoint for the strong content intervention.
It does not rule out generative video models, explicit planning, learned
time-to-go, hierarchical subgoal conversion, other VLA backbones, or real-robot
benefit.  TG4 must complete before the LaWAM result can be assigned to a
training component.

Predicted futures should therefore be evaluated as control inputs, not only as
representations.  A publishable claim requires matched training utility,
visible task-level safety, and fixed-checkpoint dependence on correct content;
source attribution requires another matched intervention.  Applying this
standard reveals both a strong positive case and two informative boundaries,
without treating either as a universal verdict on world-model-augmented VLAs.
