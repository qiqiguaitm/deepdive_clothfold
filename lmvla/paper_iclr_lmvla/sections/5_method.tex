\section{Experimental Design}\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Benchmark, evaluator, and evidence tiers}
RoboTwin 2.0 is the primary benchmark.  We evaluate hammering, ranking by
color, ranking by size, handover, stacking two blocks, and stacking three
blocks.  Each completed pilot cell contains four simulator seed groups and 50
accepted expert-feasible episodes per group (200 episodes per task and 1,200
per method).  Policies predict 50 actions and replan after executing 50.
Accepted scene identifiers must be intersected across conditions for paired
tests.  LIBERO is reported only as an auxiliary saturation and negative-transfer
diagnostic.

We separate three evidence tiers.  The \emph{external calibration} is the
published $\pi_{0.5}$ checkpoint evaluated through our bridge.  The
\emph{legacy pilot} starts every arm from \texttt{pi05\_base} but uses
joint-delta actions, quantile normalization, batch 64, and 20k updates.  The
\emph{confirmatory matrix} uses absolute actions, mean/std normalization,
batch 16, 50k updates, and the published optimizer schedule for every arm.
Only the confirmatory matrix can support the final Q1 claim.

\subsection{Q1: matched control performance}
The claim-bearing comparison is A0 (no hint), A2-Abs (offline absolute
milestone), and A3/MINT-VLA (current-encoder residual predictor), trained from
the same initialization and data under the confirmatory recipe.  A0 has no
hint field, milestone loss, or milestone-specific parameters.  We report each
training seed, the seed mean and interval, and all task-level results.

All nine A0/A2-Abs/A3 checkpoints and evaluations pass the frozen checkpoint,
configuration, dataset, 24-cell scene-manifest, launch-provenance, and source
audits.  Every arm uses training seeds 1000--1002 and 1,200 frozen-scene
rollouts per seed.  Paired hierarchical intervals use training seed as the
top-level resampling unit and preserve episode pairing within task and scene.

\subsection{Q2: effect of the predicted token content}
For each trained A2-Abs and A3 checkpoint, we hold weights fixed and replace the
correct prediction with (i) the current-state feature, (ii) zeros, (iii) a
within-task shuffled future, and (iv) a cross-task future.  A completed
feature-dimension permutation is reported separately because it tests feature
structure, not instance relevance.  Conditions use the
same accepted scene identifiers.  Correct-versus-current tests temporal content;
correct-versus-shuffled tests instance relevance while preserving a plausible
feature distribution; zero tests removal of the token; cross-task tests semantic
misdirection.  We report paired success differences and exact McNemar tests
with Holm correction.  The completed $\pi_{0.5}$ intervention uses the common
intersection of 518 scenes for every A2 and A3 condition.  A supporting LaWAM
diagnosis uses 1,200 paired episodes for each of nine method--control contrasts.

\subsection{Q3: privileged spatial action-utility gate}
To test whether global pooling alone removes useful geometry, we preserve the
current encoder's $4\!\times\!4$ patch topology and compare three
parameter-matched conditions: no goal, current-image patches, and the
privileged future milestone.  All conditions train for 1,000 updates on fixed
Stack-3 and Hammer splits; evaluation uses 40 held-out episodes per task and
four frozen frames per episode.  Endpoint L2 error is the prespecified primary
offline action-flow measure.  Predictor expansion is allowed only if the
privileged condition beats both controls on Stack-3 and does not regress
Hammer.  This gate tests whether the supplied future representation is usable
by the adapter before adding prediction error; it is not a closed-loop success
experiment.

\subsection{Oracle stage-transition follow-up}
We separately test whether privileged task-stage information is sufficient for
closed-loop control.  MT1 aligns the current joint state monotonically to
automatically mined task-local transition boundaries along the same-scene
expert trajectory and routes the resulting oracle transition through the
action expert.  The three independent training seeds use the corrected
50,000-update recipe and the same 24-cell, 1,200-episode evaluation manifest as
A0.  The predeclared gate requires a positive paired hierarchical difference
whose 95\% interval excludes zero, together with improvements on at least two
of Handover, Stack-2, and Stack-3.  This experiment tests a privileged oracle
upper bound; it does not test an automatic stage tracker.

\subsection{Privileged semantic-subtask follow-up}
We test a second task-stage upper bound without changing policy weights.  A
fixed public $\pi_{0.5}$ checkpoint receives no subtask, a generic stage, the
current semantic subtask, the correct next semantic subtask, or a within-task
shuffled next subtask.  All five conditions use the same 24 cells and 240
episodes.  The predeclared gate requires the paired episode-bootstrap 95\%
lower bound for correct-next minus no-subtask success to exceed zero, a higher
correct-next point estimate than generic-stage, semantic-current, and shuffled
semantics, and no task regression greater than 5 points.  This experiment
tests privileged prompting of an unchanged policy; it does not evaluate a
learned semantic predictor.

\subsection{Pretrained-policy-preserving predictive branch}
The completed negative MINT-VLA experiment motivates a separate hypothesis
rather than a post-hoc repair of the same method.  The new branch preserves the
official $\pi_{0.5}$ visual-language and action paths, stops the predictive loss
at every inherited feature, and routes a zero-initialized action-conditioned
adapter only into the action expert.  Its offline P0 gate compares normal,
shuffled, and masked action conditioning on held-out latent prediction; this
gate can authorize control training but cannot establish utility.

P1 trains a current-source A0 and the predictive candidate at seed 1000 under
matched data, normalization, optimizer, update count, and source identity.  At
the final checkpoint, normal inference is compared on the same 24 cells with
A0, a zeroed route, shuffled action conditioning, and masked actions.  The
directional screen requires the normal candidate to exceed every control and
permits no task regression larger than five points.  P2 is a separate
replication gate: seeds 1001 and 1002 must finish the frozen recipe, and the
hierarchical 95\% interval for candidate minus matched A0 must exclude zero
before we claim a replicated utility effect.

R1 adds dense recurrence-derived CRAVE targets to the predictive branch.  Its
necessary gate compares the combined model with A0, the parent predictive
adapter, CRAVE-only training, zero-route, and shuffled-action controls.  A
failed R1 gate rejects this auxiliary extension; it does not retroactively
negate a P1 result.

\subsection{Q4: robustness, scope, and efficiency}
Training seed is the top-level uncertainty unit.  We will use a hierarchical
bootstrap over training seed, task, and paired scene, while retaining task-level
values to expose heterogeneous effects.  Transfer is tested by matched
fine-tuning from a mature initialization, not by comparing against an unrelated
public checkpoint.  The task-scope hypothesis is predeclared on block and bowl
stacking, with reactive/contact and fine-grained geometric tasks as controls.
We report added parameters, training FLOPs, peak memory, inference latency, and
throughput relative to A0.

Training-seed replication and the paired deployment-cost benchmark are
complete.  The positive matched-utility gate failed, so mature-initialization
and predeclared task-group expansions are not run; this prevents post-hoc
selector tuning against the confirmatory result.

\subsection{Q5: instantiation on a second VLA architecture}
The milestone target and predictor are defined independently of the
$\pi_{0.5}$ prefix.  A future test with another architecture would retain the
milestone selection, native-space objective, data, and evaluation protocol;
size the predictor to the second VLA's visual feature width; and connect it
through that VLA's native conditioning input.  The identifying comparison is
MINT-VLA versus a matched no-milestone baseline within the second architecture,
not raw success differences between two VLA families.  We do not make a
multi-architecture claim and close this expansion after the primary
$\pi_{0.5}$ utility gate fails.
